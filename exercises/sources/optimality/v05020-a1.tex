

\begin{enumerate}
\item 
  The gradient is
\[
  \nabla f(x)= \begin{pmatrix*} 100 x_1
    \\ -3x_2^2 \end{pmatrix*}, \; \nabla f(x^*)= \cvect{0 \\ 0}.
  \]
  Therefore, the first order necessary condition is verified.

  The second derivative matrix is
  \[
  \nabla^2 f(x)= \begin{pmatrix*} 100 & 0\\ 0 &
    -6x_2 \end{pmatrix*}, \; \nabla^2 f(x^*)= \begin{pmatrix*} 100 & 0\\ 0 &
    0 \end{pmatrix*}.
  \]
It is positive semidefinite. Indeed, the eigenvalues are read on the
diagonal, as it is a diagonal matrix. And they are non
negative. Therefore,  the second order necessary condition
is verified.

\item Note that the value of the objective function at $x^*$ is
  \[
f(x^*) = 0.
  \]
  Consider the direction
  \[
  d_1=\cvect{0 \\ 1}
  \]
  and the point $x_1$ obtained by following this direction from $x^*$
  with a step
  $\alpha$:
  \[
x_1 = x^* + \alpha d_1 = \cvect{0 \\ 0} + \alpha \cvect{0 \\ 1} =
\cvect{0 \\ \alpha}.
\]
The value of the objective function at $x_1$ is
\[
f(x_1) = -\alpha^3.
\]
Therefore, for any $\alpha > 0$, we have
\[
f(x_1) < f(x^*), 
\]
and $x^*$ cannot be a local minimum. 

  Consider now the direction
  \[
  d_2=\cvect{0 \\ -1}
  \]
  and the point $x_2$ obtained by following this direction from $x^*$
  with a step
  $\alpha$:
  \[
x_2 = x^* + \alpha d_2 = \cvect{0 \\ 0} + \alpha \cvect{0 \\ -1} =
\cvect{0 \\ -\alpha}.
\]
The value of the objective function at $x_2$ is
\[
f(x_2) = \alpha^3.
\]
Therefore, for any $\alpha > 0$, we have
\[
f(x_2) > f(x^*), 
\]
and $x^*$ cannot be a local maximum. 

\end{enumerate}

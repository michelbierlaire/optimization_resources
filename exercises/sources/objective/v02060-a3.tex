
The Jacobian matrix is the transposed of the gradient matrix. In the
gradient matrix, each row corresponds to a variable, and each column
to a function. For the Jacobian matrix, it is the opposite. Here, as
we have 2 variables and 3 functions, we have
\[
\nabla f(x) \in \R^{2 \times 3}, \;\;J(x) \in \R^{3\times 2}.
\]
The gradient matrix is
\[
\nabla f(x)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
   \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial x_1}      & \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial x_1}  & \frac{\partial f_3}{\partial x_1} \\*[1.2ex]
    \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial x_2}       & \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial x_2}
 & \frac{\partial f_3}{\partial x_2}\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
   2x_1x_2      & -\sin(e^{x_1+x_2^2})e^{x_1+x_2^2} & 0\\
    x_1^2      & -2x_2\sin(e^{x_1+x_2^2})e^{x_1+x_2^2} & 1
    \end{pmatrix}.
\]

The Jacobian matrix is
\[
   J(x)=\nabla f(x)^T
=
\begin{pmatrix}
   \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial x_1}      & \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial x_2} \\*[1.2ex]
    \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial x_1}       & \frac{\partial
      f_2}{\partial x_2} \\*[1.2ex]
    \frac{\partial f_3}{\partial x_1}       & \frac{\partial
      f_3}{\partial x_2} 
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
   2x_1x_2      &   x_1^2 \\
    -\sin(e^{x_1+x_2^2})e^{x_1+x_2^2}    &
    -2x_2\sin(e^{x_1+x_2^2})e^{x_1+x_2^2} \\
    0 & 1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

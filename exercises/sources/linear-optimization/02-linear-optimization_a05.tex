\subsubsection*{Question 1}
\begin{align*}
x_1+2x_2+3x_3 & = 0,\\
4x_1+5x_2+6x_3 & = 0,\\
7x_1+8x_2& =0.
\end{align*}
We have 3 equations and 3 variables. In matrix form, we have
\[
Ax = b, \text{ with } A= \begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3\\ 4&5&6\\ 7&8&0
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad b = \begin{pmatrix}
0\\0\\0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We transform the matrix into a row echelon form to identify its rank
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 0 
\end{pmatrix}
\stackrel{\mathclap{(1)}}{=}
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & -3 & -6 \\ 0 & -6 & -21 
\end{pmatrix}
\stackrel{\mathclap{(2)}}{=}
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & -3 & -6 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 
\end{pmatrix}
\]
where the following elementary row operations have been applied:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] $r_3 = r_3 - 7r_1$ and $r_2=r_2-4r_1$.
\item[(2)] $r_3 = r_3 -2 r_2$.
\end{itemize}
Note that to obtain a true row echelon form, we would also need to normalize
each row to obtain a one as the first leading element. However, it is
not necessary in order to obtain the rank of the matrix, which is the number of
non zero rows in this representation. As each row contains non
zero elements, the rank is 3 here.

The system is compatible, as the vector
\[
x=\cvect{0 \\ 0 \\ 0}
\]
is a solution of the system. As the matrix is of full rank, there is
no redundant equation, and $x$ is the unique solution.


\subsubsection*{Question 2}
\begin{align*}
x_1+2x_2+3x_3 & = 0,\\
4x_1+5x_2+6x_3 & = 0,\\
7x_1+8x_2+9x_3 & =0.
\end{align*}
We have 3 equations and 3 variables. In matrix form, we have
\[
Ax = b, \text{ with } A= \begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3\\ 4&5&6\\ 7&8&9
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad b = \begin{pmatrix}
0\\0\\0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We transform the matrix into a row echelon form to identify its rank
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 
\end{pmatrix}
\stackrel{\mathclap{(1)}}{=}
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & -3 & -6 \\ 0 & -6 & -12 
\end{pmatrix}
\stackrel{\mathclap{(2)}}{=}
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & -3 & -6 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 
\end{pmatrix}
\]
where the following row operations have been applied:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] $r_3 = r_3 - 7r_1$ and $r_2=r_2-4r_1$.
\item[(2)] $r_3 = r_3-2 r_2$.
\end{itemize}
As only two rows contain non zero elements, the rank of the matrix is 2. 
The system is compatible, as the vector
\[
x=\cvect{0 \\ 0 \\ 0}
\]
is a solution of the system. Therefore, one of the constraints is
redundant, and there is an infinite number of solutions.

\subsubsection*{Question 3}
\begin{align*}
x_1+2x_2+3x_3 & = 0,\\
2x_1+4x_2+6x_3 & =0,\\
5x_1+10x_2+15x_3 & =0.
\end{align*}

We have 3 equations and 3 variables. In matrix form, we have
\[
Ax = b, \text{ with } A= \begin{pmatrix}
1&2&3\\ 2&4&6\\ 5&10&15
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad b = \begin{pmatrix}
0\\0\\0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We transform the matrix into a row echelon form to identify its rank
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 5 & 10 & 15 
\end{pmatrix}
\stackrel{\mathclap{(1)}}{=}
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 
\end{pmatrix}
\]
where the following row operations have been applied:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] $r_3 = r_3 - 5r_1$ and $r_2=r_2-2r_1$.
\end{itemize}
As only one row contains non zero elements, the rank of the matrix is 1. 
The system is compatible, as the vector
\[
x=\cvect{0 \\ 0 \\ 0}
\]
is a solution of the system. Therefore, two of the constraints are
redundant, and there is an infinite number of solutions.

\subsubsection*{Question 4}
\begin{align*}
x_1 + x_2 + x_3 &= 1,\\
x_1 - x_2 + x_4 &= 1, \\
x_1 -5x_2 -2x_3 +3x_4 &= 1.
\end{align*}

We have 3 equations and 4 variables. In matrix form, we have
\[
Ax = b, \text{ with } A= \begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&0\\ 1&-1&0&1\\ 1&-5&-2&3
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad b = \begin{pmatrix}
1\\1\\1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We transform the matrix into a row echelon form to identify its rank
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1  & -1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1  & -5 & -2 & 3  
\end{pmatrix}
\stackrel{\mathclap{(1)}}{=}
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & -6 & -3 & 3
\end{pmatrix}
\stackrel{\mathclap{(2)}}{=}
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 
\end{pmatrix}
\]
where the following row operations have been applied:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] $r_3 = r_3 - r_1$ and $r_2=r_2-r_1$.
\item[(2)] $r_3 = r_3 -3 r_2$.
\end{itemize}
As only two rows contain non zero elements, the rank of the matrix is 2. 
The system is compatible, as the vector
\[
x=\cvect{1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0}
\]
is a solution of the system. Therefore, one of the constraints is
redundant, and there is an infinite number of solutions.

\subsubsection*{Question 5}

\begin{align*}
x_1+x_2&=0,\\
x_1+2x_2+2x_3&=0,\\
3x_2+3x_3&=0.
\end{align*}

We have 3 equations and 3 variables. In matrix form, we have
\[
Ax = b, \text{ with } A= \begin{pmatrix}
1&1&0\\ 1&2&2\\ 0&3&3
\end{pmatrix}, \qquad b = \begin{pmatrix}
0\\0\\0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
We transform the matrix into a row echelon form to identify its rank
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1  & 2 & 2 \\ 0  & 3 & 3  
\end{pmatrix}
\stackrel{\mathclap{(1)}}{=}
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2  \\ 0 & 3 & 3
\end{pmatrix}
\stackrel{\mathclap{(2)}}{=}
\begin{pmatrix}
  1 & 1  & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2  \\ 0 & 0 & -3
\end{pmatrix}
\]
where the following row operations have been applied:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] $r_2=r_2-r_1$.
\item[(2)] $r_3 = r_3 -3 r_2$.
\end{itemize}
As each row contains non
zero elements, the rank of the matrix is 3. 
The system is compatible, as the vector
\[
x=\cvect{0 \\ 0 \\ 0}
\]
is a solution of the system. Therefore, no constraint is
redundant, and $x$ is a unique solution.
